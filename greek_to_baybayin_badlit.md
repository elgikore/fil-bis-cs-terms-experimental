> [!NOTE]
>
> I just realized that AIU vowels in the original Baybayin makes a lot of sense when you see many spelling variations in modern Filipino is swapping e to i or o to u and vice versa. It is even more apparent in Bisaya, as [John Wolff transcribed it](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/40074/pg40074-images.html).
> > "We write only three vowels: i, u, a. In Cebuano publications /i/ is sometimes written i, sometimes e, but with no consistency; and /u/ sometimes is written u, sometimes o (again with no consistency). But here the letters e and o are not used." 
>
> This makes sense when you realize the official spelling for structure in Filipino is istraktura, and for Bisaya, it is estraktura.

# Lowercase Greek
> [!NOTE]
>
> E = I; O = U

* **ᜀ** = **α**
    * From **A**lpha
* **ᜊᜒ** = **β**
    * From **Be**ta (Be = Bi)
* **ᜄ** = **γ**
    * From **Ga**mma
* **ᜇᜒ** = **δ**
    * From **De**lta (De = Di)
* **ᜂ** = **ε**
    * By similarity
* **ᜐᜒ** = **ζ**
    * **Ze**ta -> **Se**ta (Se = Si)
* **ᜁ** = **η**
    * **E**ta -> **I**ta
* **ᜆᜒ** = **θ**
    * **The**ta -> **Te**ta (Te = Ti)
* **ᜎ** = **λ**
    * From **La**mda
* **ᜌᜓ** = **μ**
    * By *yu* sound of mu
* **ᜅᜓ** = **ν**
    * By *new* sound of nu, which sounds like *ngu* as in ngunit
* **ᜐ** = **ξ**
    * **Kza**i (pronounciation) -> **Sa**i (transliteration)
* **ᜉ** = **π** (anything that is not 3.14 like in statistics)
    * By *pa* (pai) sound of pi
* **ᜍᜓ** = ρ
    * **Rh**o -> **Ro** (Ro = Ru)
* **ᜅ** = σ
    * Si**gma** -> Si**gna** (gna is reinterpreted as nga by sound similarity)
* **ᜆ** = **τ**
    * From **Ta**u
* **ᜑ** = **ϕ**
    * P**hi** -> F**hay** (by transliteration) -> F**ha**y
    * F = P by convention
* **ᜃ** = **χ** 
    * By *kai* sound of chi
* **ᜌ** = **ψ**
    * P**si** -> P**say** (by transliteration) -> Ps**ay** (ay gets reinterpreted as ya)
* **ᜋᜒ** = **ω**
    * From O**me**ga (Me = Mi) and also o**mni** (interpreted as mi)
